# Wink messaging

This file turns the approved strategy into product language, UX copy and pitch-ready messages. It intentionally excludes the visual identity direction section from the seven-things scope.

The supplied brief describes a concept product. Use the status labels below when publishing claims:

- **Shipped:** evidenced as implemented and exercised. None is evidenced in the supplied brief.
- **Target:** part of the intended first release.
- **Roadmap:** deliberately deferred.
- **Hypothesis:** requires real-user or market validation.

## Message control

Lead with the human action. Explain the chain, wallet or contract only when it helps the user understand the action or its evidence. Never use a planned feature as proof that the product already works.

## Category and boundary

**Wink is a personal on-chain gifting and social value transfer.**

It is not a general wallet, exchange, custody service or universal solution. It performs the narrow action described in the target first release.

## Core message

Wink turns a native BOT transfer into a personal, wallet-bound gift that the recipient can open and claim.

## Value proposition

Wink gives a BOT transfer a sender, a message, a reveal and a recipient-bound claim.

**For the user:** The sender feels thoughtful. The recipient understands why the transfer arrived and has a small moment to open.

**For the surrounding community:** The sender can express thanks, celebration or encouragement without asking the recipient to understand a trading interface.

**Why believe it:** The target GiftVault flow binds the gift to a recipient wallet, locks native BOT until claim, prevents a second claim and permits reclaim only after expiry. The source brief also requires clear explanation that the recipient needs BOT for gas.

## Messaging house

### Roof

Wink gives a BOT transfer a sender, a message, a reveal and a recipient-bound claim.

### Pillar 1: a clear human action

Tell the user what to do in ordinary language. Do not ask them to understand the whole network before they can understand the first step.

### Pillar 2: a visible result

Show the receipt, claim state, evidence label or next action that follows the product event. Do not flatten pending, confirmed, claimed, reviewed and unresolved into one word.

### Pillar 3: an honest boundary

Make the product's limit easy to repeat. The product should say what its evidence supports and what it does not support.

Wink turns a native BOT transfer into a personal, wallet-bound gift that the recipient can open and claim. It keeps the proof visible and the claim limited to what the product actually checked.

## Full narrative

People currently face this problem: a normal crypto transfer is functional but emotionally flat. The recipient may see an unfamiliar transaction without knowing why it was sent or what to do next. The usual workaround creates a gap between what happened and what another person can understand. Wink closes that gap through a narrow product action, a clear state model and a public result. The narrow product focuses on a nominated wallet, a shareable reveal page, a short off-chain note and a clear expiry and reclaim path. It is not an anonymous bearer-link system or a gasless wallet-creation product. The product is useful only if it tells the truth about its own evidence, so the first release must show both the result and the boundary.

## Feature-to-value translations

- **Wallet connection and network check** → the product knows which network and account are active → the user avoids signing in the wrong context → the action feels deliberate.
- **Narrow contract or proof rule** → the product can enforce the defined boundary → the user knows what is included → the product feels dependable.
- **State-specific result** → the product shows what happened and what remains unknown → the user avoids duplicate actions → the product reduces anxiety.
- **Public receipt or evidence page** → another person can inspect the relevant reference → the user can explain the action without translating raw wallet data → the product becomes easier to share.

## Voice rules

### 1. Start with the action

Say what the user can do before naming the infrastructure.

- Before: "Access a next-generation on-chain experience."
- After: "Choose the recipient, add a note and review the expiry."

### 2. Show the evidence boundary

Name the exact thing a transaction, claim or review proves.

- Before: "This makes the project trustworthy."
- After: "This confirms the supported action. It does not rate the project’s safety."

### 3. Be warm without becoming vague

Use the emotional register of the product, but keep the user’s next action visible.

- Before: "A delightful experience for the future of value."
- After: "Your gift is ready to claim." if the state is confirmed.

## Cadence and sentence rules

- Use short sentences for actions and errors.
- Put the important noun near the verb: recipient, amount, gift, action, review, receipt.
- Use one claim per sentence.
- Keep technical terms in supporting copy unless the user must act on them.
- Do not use a slogan where a label is required.

## Vocabulary bank

Prefer: agreed, share, recipient, note, gift, claim, use, review, evidence, supported, pending, confirmed, unresolved, receipt, explorer.

Use carefully: verified, real, secure, trusted, complete, successful. Add the object and scope whenever one of these words appears.

Avoid: seamless, revolutionary, frictionless, trustless, guaranteed, safe, audited, best, universal, instant, autonomous, yield, alpha, moon, ecosystem solution.

- **Title:** Review before you sign

### Pending

- **Title:** Waiting for confirmation
- **Body:** Your wallet has sent the request. Do not repeat the action while the result is unresolved.

### Rejected

- **Title:** Nothing was confirmed
- **Body:** The wallet rejected the request. Review the details and try again when you are ready.
- **Action:** Try again

### Failed

- **Title:** The action failed
- **Body:** The network did not confirm this action. Check the details before retrying.
- **Action:** Review and retry

### Confirmed

- **Title:** Confirmed
- **Body:** The transaction is confirmed. Open the receipt to see the result.
- **Action:** View receipt

### Unknown outcome

- **Title:** We are still checking
- **Body:** We do not know the final result yet. Do not resend. Check the transaction reference or try again after reconciliation.

### Claimable

- **Title:** Your gift is ready
- **Body:** Connect the nominated wallet to review the gift and claim it. You will need BOT for gas.
- **Action:** Claim the gift

### Claimed

- **Title:** Gift claimed
- **Body:** The nominated wallet claimed the gift. Open the receipt to see the confirmed transaction.
- **Action:** View receipt

### Expired

- **Title:** This gift has expired
- **Body:** The claim window ended before the gift was claimed. The sender can review the reclaim state.

### Reclaimable

- **Title:** Gift ready to reclaim
- **Body:** The expiry has passed and the gift remains unclaimed. Confirm the reclaim action before signing.
- **Action:** Reclaim gift

## Objection responses

### "Is this custodial?"

The intended flow uses connected wallets and a target contract or verification boundary. The product should not describe itself as custody unless the implementation genuinely holds or controls user assets.

### "Does this prove the other side is safe or trustworthy?"

No. The product should state exactly what it checked. A transaction, claim or qualifying action can support a narrow event claim without proving quality, safety, bias or future performance.

### "What happens if the transaction fails?"

The product keeps the failed state separate from confirmation, explains the next safe action and avoids telling the user to repeat an unresolved transaction.

- **Claim:** The result is confirmed. **Status:** Shipped only after the relevant chain state confirms it. **Proof required:** Transaction reference, event or equivalent product evidence.

## Testing plan

Test the language with people who are not familiar with the source brief.

1. Ask a stranger to explain what Wink does after reading the first screen.
2. Ask which action they would take next.
3. Ask what the product proves and what it does not prove.
4. Test the pending and failure language with a wallet user.
5. Compare the primary headline with the one-line description and remove any version that sounds like a generic crypto product.

**Acceptance criteria:** A new user can name the category, first action, result and evidence boundary without reading the full page.
